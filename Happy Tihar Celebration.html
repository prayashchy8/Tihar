Festival Significance

The 5-Day Festival of Lights

Tihar, also known as Deepawali or Yamapanchak, is one of the grandest Hindu festivals celebrated predominantly in Nepal and parts of India. Over five days, it honors the divine bond between humans, animals, nature, and gods.

Houses are decorated with colorful marigold garlands (Sayapatri), glittering oil lamps (Diyas), intricate Rangolis, and bright LED lights to welcome Goddess Laxmi—the deity of wealth and prosperity.

The 5 Days of Tihar

Click on each day to explore the rituals and cultural significance.

Day 1 🐦

Kaag Tihar

Worship of Crows

Crows are revered as messengers of Yama, the god of death. Delicacies and grains are placed on roofs for them.

Day 2 🐕

Kukur Tihar

Worship of Dogs

Dogs are honored for their loyalty and guardianship with garlands of marigolds, red Tika, and delicious food.

Read More
Day 3 🐄

Gai Tihar & Laxmi Puja

Worship of Cows & Goddess Laxmi

In the morning cows are worshipped. Evening brings Laxmi Puja, lighting diyas to welcome wealth and prosperity.

Read More
Day 4 🐂

Govardhan Puja & Mha Puja

Worship of Oxen & Self-Worship

Oxen are worshipped, and the Newar community celebrates Mha Puja (worship of oneself and soul purification).

Read More
Day 5 🌺

Bhai Tika

Sister-Brother Bonding

Sisters apply Seven-Color Tika (Saptarangi Tika) and garlands (Makhamali) on their brothers' foreheads, wishing long life.
